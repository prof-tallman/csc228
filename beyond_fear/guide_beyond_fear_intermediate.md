# Beyond Fear: Reading Guide for Select Chapters #

## Overall Guiding Question ##

**Overall Guiding Question: How do technology, complexity, and identity systems create new security strengths and weaknesses?**

## Focus Points for Intermediate Chapters ##

### 7. Technology and Security Imbalances ###

This chapter explains how technology can change the balance between attackers and defenders. Schneier shows that new technologies can solve old problems while also creating new vulnerabilities, new forms of complexity, and new opportunities for attack. As you read, pay attention to why technology does not automatically make people safer. Look especially for:

- Why new technology often favors one side initially, before the other adapts
- Why attackers often need to find only one useful weakness
- Why defenders must protect many parts of a system
- How complexity can create failures that are hard to predict
- How simple systems can become more complicated through technology
- The advantages and disadvantages of standardization
- How one attack can affect many similar systems
- How distance and jurisdiction can change the consequences of an attack

Why does technology make security both stronger and more fragile at the same time?

### 13. Identification, Authentication, and Authorization ###

This chapter explains how security systems decide who someone is, whether they can prove it, and what they are allowed to do. Schneier shows that identity systems can fail when credentials are easy to copy, hard to change, or used in the wrong context. As you read, pay attention to the difference between identifying a person, authenticating a claim, and authorizing an action. Look especially for:

- The difference between identification, authentication, and authorization
- Authentication based on what you know
- Authentication based on what you have
- Authentication based on what you are
- The characteristics of poor authenticators
- Why biometrics are attractive but limited
- Why rare threats can produce many false alarms even in accurate systems
- Why credentials that cannot be changed create special problems

Why is proving identity more complicated than it first appears?
