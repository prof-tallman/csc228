# Technology, Security, and Fear #

This course was written as part of [Concordia University Irvine's *Enduring Questions and Ideas (Q&I)* General Education Curriculum](https://www.cui.edu/about/enduring-questions-ideas/) and addresses the enduring question, **"How Shall I Live?"**

## Course Overview ##

How should we live wisely in a world shaped by technology, dangers, and fear? In this course, we will explore that question through stories, films, discussions, and hands-on activities. At a generally accessible level, we will learn how the internet works, how cryptography has shaped history, how hackers and cyber defenders think, and how simple codes can be made and broken. Along the way, we will ask whether security measures truly make us safer or merely make us feel safer, weigh their costs and benefits, and consider how to live with wisdom, grace, and courage in the midst of fear.

During the course, we will enjoy special guest lectures and Q&A time with a practicing psychiatrist and a theology professor. These guests will help us understand how the human body handles fear and a biblical response to fear. We will watch movies like [*The Imitation Game*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt2084970/) and [*The Net*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0113957/?ref_=nv_sr_srsg_0_tt_8_nm_0_in_0_q_The%20Net%27) that dramatize fear in relation to the technology of their day. The readings include selections from Sir Arthur Conan Doyle's [Sherlock Holmes stories](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0385006896?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback), Simon Singh's [The Code Book](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0385495323?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback), Bruce Schneier's [Beyond Fear](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0387026207?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback), and Corrie ten Boom's [The Hiding Place](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0800794052?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback).

## Anxiety and Worry ##

I created this course with the understanding that many college students probably do not experience significant fear related to cybersecurity. Yet fear and concern often shape the laws, policies, security measures, and business practices that influence our everyday use of technology. Decisions about technology are frequently responses to perceived risks, potential harms, legal liability, and uncertainty.

So while undergraduate students may not recognize knee-knocking fear in regards to technology, they likely experience worry and anxiety in other areas of life: grades, finances, public speaking, finding a job, family responsibilities, relationships, and an uncertain future. My hope is that students will learn to evaluate risks, countermeasures, costs, and tradeoffs wisely in the context of technology, and then recognize that many of the same habits of thought can be applied to the worries and fears of everyday life.

The course is not so much about making ourselves more secure; instead, it is how we should live when complete security is impossible. My hope is that students will learn to respond to uncertainty with wisdom, courage, responsibility, and trust.
