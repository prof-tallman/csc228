# Midterm #2: Preparing for the Exam #

The exam will emphasize explanation and thoughtful argument rather than puzzle solving or memorization. It will ask you to explain important ideas in your own words, apply them to specific examples, connect technical knowledge with questions about fear, and bring together ideas from *Beyond Fear* and *The Hiding Place*.

## Overview ##

You will not know the exact questions in advance. Prepare by making sure that you understand the major ideas well enough to explain them without relying on memorized definitions or prepared paragraphs. This test covers the major ideas since the last exam.

The exam will include two parts:

- **Part I: Technical Concepts (40 points)** will ask you to explain and apply ideas from *Beyond Fear* and from our class activities about how the internet works. You will need to write several short paragraphs, each one about a different topic.
- **Part II: The Fear Essay (60 points)** will ask you to use *Beyond Fear* and *The Hiding Place* to develop a thoughtful response to a question about fear, security, judgment, and the treatment of others.

### Essay Rubric ###

| Criterion | Points |
| --------- | -----: |
| A clear answer followed by a consistent argument and supported by evidence | 15 |
| Accurate and meaningful use of *Beyond Fear*, including quotation and explanation | 15 |
| Accurate and meaningful use of *The Hiding Place*, including quotation and explanation | 15 |
| Analysis, complexity, organization, and conclusion | 15 |
| **Total** | **60** |

### Readings and Material Covered ###

- *Beyond Fear*, Chapters 1, 2, 3, and 7
- *The Hiding Place*, Chapters 1–8
- Class lessons and activities on the internet and internet security

### Quotes Page ###

You may bring one page of quotations from the two books. Your quotes page may contain quotations, page references, and brief notes identifying their context. It may not contain prepared essay paragraphs, thesis statements, or outlines. You will turn in your quotes page with your exam. Choose quotations that:

- You understand well.
- Represent important ideas or decisions.
- Could support more than one possible argument.
- Help you connect the two books.

For each quotation, know its context, what idea it expresses, and why it matters. The quotation itself will not earn much credit unless you explain how it supports your response.

You may also bring a copy of each book. However, because exam time is limited, you should arrive knowing which quotations and examples you intend to use. The books and permitted notes are references, not substitutes for preparation.

### Homework Page ###

In addition to your quotes page, you may bring one completed homework assignment from this course. The assignment must be your own work and must remain in its original form; you may not add new notes, outlines, quotations, or a prepared essay to it before the exam.

Choose a homework assignment that will help you recall important ideas, examples, or connections from the course. You may refer to it during the exam, but you will still need to explain the material in your own words and apply it to the questions you are given.

## Part I: Technical Concepts ##

Review the following ideas from *Beyond Fear*:

- The difference between a threat and a risk
- How people estimate and misjudge risk
- The competing goals and constraints of attackers versus defenders
- Complexity and its effects on security
- The costs, benefits, and tradeoffs involved in security decisions

Review the major ideas from our internet activities:

- How information is divided into packets
- How the layers of the TCP/IP model work together through encapsulation
- How DNS translates domain names into IP addresses
- How routers use the IP layer to move packets toward their destinations
- How digital certificates and certificate authorities help authenticate websites
- How the internet uses encryption to protect communications between a browser and a web server
- How TOR uses encryption to anonymize internet browsing

Be prepared to:

- Explain a concept or process clearly in your own words.
- Describe the important steps in a technical process.
- Connect an internet concept to a question about fear, risk, trust, or security.

## Part II: Essay on Fear ##

Part II will ask you to develop and defend your own position on a question involving fear, security, judgment, and the treatment of others. You must use ideas and examples from both *Beyond Fear* and *The Hiding Place*, rather than merely summarizing the books. You may write in paragraphs or as a detailed outline, but your response must include enough explanation to demonstrate your reasoning.

- How Schneier distinguishes fear, threats, risks, and security.
- How fear can lead people to misjudge danger or support ineffective or excessive security measures.
- The ideas of security theater, tradeoffs, complexity, the attacker's advantage, authentication, and trust.
- Important decisions made by Corrie, Betsie, Nollie, and others in Chapters 1–8 of *The Hiding Place*.
- Examples of people responding to fear with courage, preparation, honesty, deception, trust, love, resistance, or faith.
- Situations in which protecting oneself or one group may conflict with responsibilities toward other people.
- The principles that should guide a person's decisions when confronted by fear, uncertainty, or genuine danger.

Be prepared to:

- State a clear answer to the essay question.
- Use at least **one important idea** from *Beyond Fear*.
- Discuss at least **one specific event**, person, or decision from *The Hiding Place*.
- Use and explain at least **one quotation from each book**.
- Connect the two books rather than discussing them separately.
- Recognize a tension, limitation, cost, or reasonable alternative to your position.
- End with a principle that could guide your own future decisions.
