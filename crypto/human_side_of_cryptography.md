# Human Side of Cryptography #

## The Imitation Game ##

The 2014 film *The Imitation Game* is an interesting (and somewhat sad) historical dramatization of the British effort to crack the Enigma Machine during World War II. The story is based on a 1983 biography on Alan Turing by Andrew Hodges. It takes a number of artistic liberties but has many of the basic facts correct and probably captures at least some of the raw emotions experienced by the men and women of Bletchley Park.

After watching The Imitation Game, respond to the following prompt in a 1-2 page paper with well-developed paragraphs.

## Reflection Question ##

1. During the film, the Allied cryptographers occasionally chose not to act on intelligence gathered from Enigma because responding to every intercepted message would reveal to the Nazis that the code had been broken. This created situations where security, secrecy, and long-term strategy came into conflict with ordinary human instincts such as compassion, honesty, loyalty, fear, and the desire to protect specific people.

   Describe one moment in the film where the characters were forced to make such a tradeoff. Explain:

   - What decision was made
   - Why the decision was difficult
   - What risks were involved
   - Whether you believe the characters acted wisely

   Then connect these same ideas to a situation from your own life where you or someone around you faced a similar kind of tradeoff between safety, honesty, secrecy, convenience, loyalty, fear, or responsibility. The situation does not need to involve cryptography or technology. Explain what made the decision difficult and whether, in hindsight, you believe the response reflected wise and responsible living. If you would handle the situation differently today, explain what lessons you have learned and how they would shape your future decisions.

   If there is a guiding principle that helps you with these decisions–-perhaps a Bible verse or advice from a mentor-–please include it in your response.

   Assume that your readers have not seen the film. They certainly do not know your life story. Your writing should include enough background and context to stand on its own.
