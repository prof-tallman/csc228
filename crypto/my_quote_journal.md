# My Quote Journal #

## Ideas Worth Remembering ##

The purpose of this course is to answer the big question *How Shall I Live?* within the context of technology, security, and fear. People naturally have many fears, some of them legitimate and others that are irrational. These fears can vary significantly from person to person and depend in large part on the circumstances of their life: Mary Queen of Scots, Charles Babbage, and you are likely to have very different fears based on your place in history, your age, and your responsibilities.

The purpose of this assignment is to begin identifying and pondering ideas that will help us answer these larger questions later on in the semester. Your task is to find three quotes from the class material thus far and to spend a little bit of time reflecting on them. At least one of the quotes must come from a reading assignment and at least one of the quotes should come from a class discussion or lecture. Your quote from class can be paraphrased. Feel free to ask Prof. Tallman for help if you know the general idea but would like help refining it into a clearer or more memorable statement.

Note: I took care to write these prompts with interesting themes and some generality. I do not want you to spend hours finding The-Most-Surprising-Quote; instead, find something that fits the theme of: A-Surprising-Quote. Don't waste time finding the best quote, just find a good one that you like and go with it.

## The Quotes ##

For each quote, write a paragraph that briefly explains: (a) why the idea stood out; (b) what it suggests about technology, security, fear, or human behavior; and (c) how it connects to modern life. You just need a few sentences about each point A-C.

1. **The Surprising Quote:** Something about information security or cryptography that you found particularly surprising.
2. **The Personally Interesting Quote:** An idea that you found to be particularly interesting.
3. **The That-Aged-Well Quote:** Find a quote that is likely to still be relevant 20 years from now.
4. Add a brief introduction (before the quotes) and conclusion (after the quotes) that will help any readers of your paper understand what you are writing about. You are encouraged to return to these same thoughts for later homework assignments.
