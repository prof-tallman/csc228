# Final Exam: Preparing for the Assignment #

The "final exam" for CSC-228 will be a writing assignment that requires 3-5 pages of reflection on the course's main question:

> **How Shall I Live?**

In addition to writing a paper, you will give a short presentation during the official final exam period and complete a brief audience reflection based on your classmates' presentations. Prof. Tallman will provide specific instructions, the grading criteria, and point values for these activities at the appropriate time.

## Overview ##

The exact prompt will be provided later in the semester so that you can research and write the paper during the final week of the term. This assignment will ask you to bring together ideas from the course readings, lectures, technical material, and class discussions as you reflect on fear, security, responsibility, and faith. Your writing will be required to:

- Develop and explain at least **three commitments, principles, or central ideas** in response to the prompt.
- Engage meaningfully with at least **two course books**.
- Include at least **three specific examples** from the course readings.
- Include at least **two direct quotations** from the course readings.
- Incorporate a relevant insight from the psychology lecture, the theology lecture, or both.
- Accurately explain at least **one Christian teaching**, using a relevant biblical passage and theological idea connected to your reflection.

Prof. Tallman will attempt to create an assignment that you will find interesting and relevant. He expects you to demonstrate the same critical thinking, depth of thought, and quality of writing that you would put into a traditional essay--even if the nature of this assignment appears somewhat informal.

The rubric below shows the criteria that Prof. Tallman will use to evaluate your final paper.

| --------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------ |

| **Course Readings and Evidence** | Skillfully integrates specific examples and direct quotations from at least two course books. | **Analysis of Fear, Risk, and Security** | Insightfully examines relationships among fear, danger, risk, security, trust, technology, and human judgment. | **Organization, Voice, and Writing Quality** | The writing is engaging, purposeful, coherent, and polished. Its organization and reflective voice strengthen its ideas. Sources are smoothly integrated and consistently cited. | The writing is clear, logically organized, appropriately reflective, and carefully revised. Sources are generally integrated and cited correctly, with few distracting errors. | Organization, voice, clarity, or citation is inconsistent. Errors or awkward passages occasionally interfere with meaning. | The writing is difficult to follow, poorly organized, insufficiently revised, or frequently disrupted by errors or citation problems. |

**Note:** The eight rubric criteria add up to a maximum of 96 points. The remaining 4 points are free "completion" points that are awarded for submitting a genuine attempt to complete the assignment.
