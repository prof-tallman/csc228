# Midterm #1: Preparing for the Exam

This exam covers our survey of cryptography, from early hand ciphers to modern symmetric and public-key systems. It will assess your understanding of the major ideas, historical developments, strengths, weaknesses, and practical challenges discussed in *The Code Book*, class activities, homework, and the GPG exercises.

The exam is designed for paper and pencil. Computers, calculators, books, and electronic devices will not be allowed. Any encryption, decryption, or cryptanalysis problems will be short enough to complete with simple work by hand. Show your work so that correct reasoning and useful intermediate steps can receive partial credit.

## Overview

The exam will include four parts:

- **Part I: Multiple Choice** will cover major concepts from the readings and class.
- **Part II: Short Answer** will ask you to explain important ideas in your own words.
- **Part III: Puzzle Problems** will require you to apply several of the historical cipher methods practiced in class.
- **Part IV: Free Response** will ask you to analyze a modern cryptographic situation and explain the security protections it does and does not provide.

### Readings and Material Covered

- *The Code Book*, Chapters 1, 2, 3, 4, and 6
- Class lessons and activities on historical cryptography and cryptanalysis
- Caesar shift, rail fence, mixed-alphabet substitution, one-time pads, Enigma, AES, and public-key cryptography
- GPG activities involving keys, encryption, decryption, and digital signatures

## Topics to Review

As you study, focus on the development of cryptography and the problems each new system attempted to solve. Review:

- The basic categories and vocabulary of secret communication
- The differences between substitution and transposition systems
- The role of keys and the importance of key secrecy
- How patterns in language make some ciphers vulnerable to cryptanalysis
- How cipher designers attempted to resist older methods of attack
- How machines and computers changed both encryption and cryptanalysis
- The design, use, and eventual defeat of Enigma
- The importance of human behavior, procedures, predictable messages, and operational mistakes
- The practical strengths and limitations of one-time pads
- The key-exchange problem
- The differences between symmetric and public-key cryptography
- The purposes of public keys, private keys, encryption, decryption, and digital signatures
- How modern systems combine different cryptographic methods
- What cryptography can protect and what security problems may remain

## Preparing for the Written Questions

Be prepared to:

- Define and compare major cryptographic concepts in your own words.
- Explain why a cryptographic method is strong or weak.
- Describe how a historical system worked at a conversational level.
- Connect developments in cryptography to examples from *The Code Book*.
- Explain how human decisions can strengthen or weaken a technical system.
- Distinguish among confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and trust.
- Apply cryptographic ideas to a short practical situation.

## Preparing for the Puzzle Problems

Review the hand-encryption and decryption methods practiced in class and on homework. You should understand the procedure well enough to apply it to a short message without software.

Some homework cryptanalysis exercises required extensive counting or repeated examination of ciphertext. The exam will not require that amount of mechanical work. Problems will be scaled appropriately for a paper-and-pencil setting, but you should still understand how patterns, repetition, frequency, and context can help a cryptanalyst.
