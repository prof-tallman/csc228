# The Hiding Place: Fear, Faithfullness, and Difficult Decisions #

## Chapters 5–8 Reflection ##

Chapters 5–8 of *The Hiding Place* show Corrie ten Boom and her family moving from ordinary life into the danger of Nazi occupation and underground resistance. Political promises fail, outward safety disappears, and the suffering of Jewish neighbors becomes impossible to ignore. The ten Booms must make daily choices about truth, secrecy, courage, hospitality, resistance, and faithfulness.

After reading Chapters 5–8, respond to the following prompt in a 2–3 page paper. A typical response will be about 750–1,500 words, depending on formatting.

## Reflection Question ##

As Nazi occupation reshapes daily life, how do Corrie and the ten Boom family choose faithfulness in the face of fear? Where do they see reminders of God's care and provision, and how do they respond when protecting others requires secrecy, danger, and difficult moral choices?

In your response, discuss at least two specific examples from Chapters 5–8. You may consider questions such as:

- How does Corrie respond when outward safety disappears?
- How do the ten Booms move from concern for Jewish people to active help?
- What risks come with sheltering fugitives at the Beje?
- How do ordinary habits of hospitality, service, prayer, humor, or community help the ten Booms continue their work?
- How do Corrie and her family respond when protecting life brings difficult moral dilemmas?
- Where do they see signs of God's providence, care, or provision?
- What biblical passages or themes seem especially important for understanding their choices?

Then connect these ideas to the larger course question: **How shall I live with respect to fear and security?**

You do not need to give a final answer to that question yet. Instead, use this paper to keep developing your answer. Chapters 5–8 show that fear is not only an emotion; it also demands decisions. It raises questions about safety, honesty, loyalty, responsibility, courage, and love. What do these chapters suggest about the kind of person someone must become in order to act faithfully when fear becomes real?

You may include a brief connection to your own life, but you are not required to share anything deeply personal. If you do make a personal connection, focus on what you have learned about fear, security, truth, responsibility, courage, or wise living. Feel free to include details from the lessons on theology and/or psychology.

Assume that your readers have not read the book. Your writing should include enough background and context to stand on its own.
