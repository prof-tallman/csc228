# The Hiding Place: Reading Guide Chapters 13–16 #

## Focus Points for Chapters 13–14 ##

### 13. Provision in Ravensbrück ###

In Chapter 13, Corrie and Betsie arrive at Ravensbrück, where the conditions are harsher than anything they have faced before. The barracks are overcrowded, filthy, and frightening, yet Corrie and Betsie continue to see signs of God's care. As you read, notice how God provides for them in ways Corrie does not always recognize at first. Look especially for:

- How Scripture becomes a source of hope and ministry in the barracks
- How Betsie responds to the suffering around her
- Why Corrie struggles with Betsie's thankfulness
- How the fleas become part of a larger lesson about God's provision
- How suffering creates opportunities for ministry rather than only despair

What does Chapter 13 show about receiving God's provision in a place of fear, suffering, and humiliation?

### 14. Ministry Through Suffering ###

In Chapter 14, Corrie and Betsie continue to suffer under the brutal conditions of Ravensbrück, but their ministry among the prisoners grows stronger. Betsie's body becomes weaker, yet her vision for what God can do through their suffering becomes clearer. As you read, notice how Corrie often struggles to see what Betsie sees. Look especially for:

- How Corrie tries to remain near Betsie
- How Betsie helps Corrie see suffering differently
- How Corrie and Betsie minister to the women around them
- How Betsie imagines healing after the war
- Who Betsie dreams of helping, and why that is surprising
- How Corrie's faithfulness is real even when she struggles to understand or obey

How does Betsie's example challenge Corrie to see suffering, ministry, and forgiveness differently?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**When fear and suffering become overwhelming, how do Corrie and Betsie learn to receive God's provision, serve others, and hope for healing beyond the camp?**

## Focus Points for Chapter 15 and the Epilog ##

### 15. Forgiveness Over Fear ###

In Chapter 15, Corrie begins the painful journey back into the world outside Ravensbrück. Betsie's visions begin to take shape, but healing is not simple or automatic. Corrie must learn that forgiveness is not a feeling she can produce on her own, but an act of obedience that requires God's strength. Look especially for:

- What makes Corrie's journey home difficult
- How Betsie's vision for a place of healing begins to come true
- How Corrie's postwar work continues Betsie's hopes
- What happens when Corrie meets a former Ravensbrück guard
- How God helps Corrie do what she cannot do by her own strength

What does Chapter 15 show about forgiveness as a response to fear, suffering, and evil?

### Epilog. Healing Wounds After the War ###

The epilog shows that Corrie's story does not end when she leaves the camp. Her suffering becomes part of her witness to the world, and her ministry reaches many different kinds of wounded people. As you read, notice how forgiveness becomes both necessary and difficult after the war. Look especially for:

- What Corrie later learns about her release from Ravensbrück
- How Corrie's message reaches people in many places
- Why victims *and* perpertrators needed healing after the war
- Why ministering to former enemies was difficult
- How Corrie's story points beyond bitterness, revenge, and fear
- How forgiveness and hope become central to Corrie's answer to suffering

What does the epilog show about the long work of healing, forgiveness, and hope after fear has done real harm?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**How do Chapters 13–15 and the epilog show Corrie learning, often through Betsie's example and God's provision, to choose forgiveness over fear, bitterness, and revenge?**
