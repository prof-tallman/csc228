# The Hiding Place: Reading Guide Chapters 9–12 #

## Focus Points for Chapters 9–10 ##

### 9. When Fear Turns to Danger ###

In Chapter 9, the danger that has been looming over the Beje finally arrives. The ten Boom family is betrayed, the Nazis raid their home, and the hidden work of the underground is exposed. As you read, notice how Corrie responds when the fear she has been living with becomes immediate and real. Look especially for:

- How the raid begins and what Corrie is doing when it happens
- What the Nazi investigators find and what they fail to find
- How Corrie responds under pressure during the raid
- Whether the secret room survives a Nazi inspection
- How the Beje changes from a place of shelter into a trap for others
- How earlier habits of faith and courage are tested when the danger becomes real

What does Chapter 9 show about the difference between preparing for danger and actually facing it?

### 10. Treasure in a Prison ###

Chapter 10 follows Corrie, Father, Nollie, and many others into Scheveningen prison. The family is separated, stripped of ordinary comfort, and treated like common criminals. Yet even in prison, Corrie receives unexpected encouragement and begins to discover that God's care has not disappeared. As you read, pay attention to what is taken away and what remains. Look especially for:

- How the family members are separated from one another
- How the guards treat Corrie and the other prisoners
- How each family member responds to their situation
- How Corrie marks important days and tries to hold on to hope
- What Corrie learns about Father, Nollie, and herself by the end of the chapter
- How prison changes Corrie's outward circumstances without removing her faith
- How small acts of kindness become signs of God's care

What can prison take away from Corrie, and what can it not take away?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**When fear becomes reality and Corrie is stripped of security and comfort, how do her habits of faith, courage, and trust help to sustain her?**

## Focus Points for Chapters 11–12 ##

### 11. Witness in Darkness ###

In Chapter 11, Corrie remains in prison and is questioned by Lieutenant Rahms. Their conversations show that darkness is not only found in prison cells; it is also found in the human soul. As you read, notice how Corrie is still able to bear witness, even while she is weak, isolated, and uncertain about her future. Look especially for:

- What Lieutenant Rahms reveals about his own spiritual condition
- What question keeps Rahms from responding in faith
- How Corrie speaks to Rahms about suffering and the Gospel
- How Corrie's suffering gives her an opportunity to speak truth to someone in darkness
- How God's providence appears in unexpected people and small mercies

What does Chapter 11 show about helping someone who might seem like an enemy?

### 12. Faithfulness in Chaos ###

In Chapter 12, Corrie and Betsie are moved to Vught, where their suffering continues in new forms. They face uncertainty, forced labor, and painful news. As you read, notice how faithfulness becomes harder when Corrie has less control over her circumstances. Look especially for:

- The emotional swings Corrie and Betsie experience as their situation keeps changing
- How transfer, paperwork, and camp routines create uncertainty
- Who Jan Vogel is and how he affects Corrie so deeply
- How Betsie helps Corrie respond to betrayal
- What kind of work Corrie does in the Philips factory
- How prisoners resist evil in small but meaningful ways
- How faithfulness includes courage, forgiveness, patience, and endurance

How does Corrie begin to learn faithfulness when she cannot control what happens next?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**When Corrie has little control over her circumstances, how does she respond faithfully to the people and responsibilities that God has placed before her?**
