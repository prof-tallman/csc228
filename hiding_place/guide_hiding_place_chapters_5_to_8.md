# The Hiding Place: Reading Guide Chapters 5-8 #

## Focus Points for Chapters 5-6 ##

### 5. When Security Fails ###

Notice how quickly ordinary life can change. The Dutch people were promised peace, but the Nazis invaded almost immediately. Holland was defeated quickly, and the ten Boom family had to learn what life under occupation meant. As you read, pay attention to the way political, national, and personal forms of security and freedom begin to fail. Look especially for:

- How quickly Holland is defeated but ordinary life continues
- Why business at the watch shop initially improves
- How the hidden radio becomes a risk for Corrie and her family
- Corrie's reaction after lying to protect the radio
- How Betsie and a simple desire for tea keep Corrie from danger
- How Nazi ideology begins to affect some Dutch citizens, not only German soldiers
- What happens to Mr. DeVries's bulldogs and what that event reveals

What does Corrie begin to learn about living faithfully when outward safety disappears?

### 6. Choosing to Help ###

As the story continues, the ten Boom family moves from concern to action. Jewish families are no longer facing only public humiliation or discrimination; they are beginning to disappear and the danger has become impossible to ignore. As you read, notice how the ten Booms respond when helping others becomes risky, illegal, and personally costly. Look especially for:

- How the danger facing Jewish families changes in this chapter
- How ordinary people in the neighborhood respond to Jewish suffering
- Why Peter is sent to prison and what his actions reveal about resistance
- How the ten Boom family's earlier reputation affects who comes to them for help
- What practical problems come with hiding extra people in the house and how Corrie solves them
- How Father responds to the possibility of being punished for helping Jewish people
- How faith shapes the ten Boom family's understanding of responsibility

What changes when fear is no longer distant or theoretical, but becomes attached to real people standing at the door?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**As Nazi occupation begins to reshape daily life, how do the ten Booms choose faithfulness in the face of fear, and where do they see reminders of God’s care and provision?**

## Focus Points for Chapters 7-8 ##

### 7. The Hidden Room and the Underground ###

In Chapter 7, the ten Boom family becomes more deeply involved in the underground resistance. Their home changes from a place of temporary help into a more permanent “hiding place” for people who have nowhere else to go. As you read, notice how the work of hiding fugitives requires courage, planning, secrecy, moral reflection, and even humor. Look especially for:

- How Corrie and her family become connected to the underground resistance
- Why some fugitives become more permanent residents at the Beje
- Whom the ten Boom family welcomes into their home, especially when others cannot take them in
- How the secret room is built and why it is hidden behind Corrie’s bedroom
- How the household practices hiding and experiences peace during dangerous raids
- How Corrie resolves the tension that sometimes develops between truth and love
- How the Beje becomes both a home of safety and a place of danger

What does Chapter 7 show about the practical, moral, and spiritual challenges of protecting people in danger?

### 8. When Truth and Love Seem to Conflict ###

In many situations, truth and love walk happily together, hand in hand. During dark times, like the ones recounted in *The Hiding Place*, the two can seem to betray each other. Chapter 8 shows Corrie and her family facing increasingly difficult choices as the danger surrounding the Beje grows. Their work with the underground brings them into contact with police, informants, raids, imprisonment, and rescue attempts. As you read, notice the courage and cost involved in protecting people in danger. Look especially for:

- How Nollie's commitment to the truth creates danger for herself and Annaliese
- How Corrie responds when the work of protecting life brings even harder moral dilemmas
- How informants, spies, and traps make the underground work more dangerous
- How Jop is caught and what his capture reveals about the risks of resistance
- How Corrie and the others continue their work despite fear, uncertainty, and grief
- How faithfulness becomes more complicated as the danger increases

When truth, love, safety, and responsibility seem to pull in different directions, how do Corrie and her family decide what faithfulness requires?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**When helping others requires secrecy, danger, and difficult moral choices, how do Corrie and her family seek to remain faithful without losing truth, love, or joy?**
