# The Hiding Place: Reading Guide Chapters 5-8 #

## Focus Points for Chapters 5-6 ##

### 5. When Security Fails ###

Notice how quickly ordinary life can change. The Dutch people were promised peace, but the Nazis invaded almost immediately. Holland was defeated quickly, and the ten Boom family had to learn what life under occupation meant. As you read, pay attention to the way political, national, and personal forms of security and freedom begin to fail. Look especially for:

- How quickly Holland is defeated but ordinary life continues
- Why business at the watch shop initially improves
- How the hidden radio becomes a risk for Corrie and her family
- Corrie's reaction after lying to protect the radio
- How Betsie and a simple desire for tea keep Corrie from danger
- How Nazi ideology begins to affect some Dutch citizens, not only German soldiers
- What happens to Mr. DeVries's bulldogs and what that event reveals

What does Corrie begin to learn about living faithfully when outward safety disappears?

### 6. Choosing to Help ###

As the story continues, the ten Boom family moves from concern to action. Jewish families are no longer facing only public humiliation or discrimination; they are beginning to disappear and the danger has become impossible to ignore. As you read, notice how the ten Booms respond when helping others becomes risky, illegal, and personally costly. Look especially for:

- How the danger facing Jewish families changes in this chapter
- How ordinary people in the neighborhood respond to Jewish suffering
- Why Peter is sent to prison and what his actions reveal about resistance
- How the ten Boom family's earlier reputation affects who comes to them for help
- Why Jewish fugitives know they can seek refuge at the Beje
- What practical problems come with hiding extra people in the house
- How Corrie obtains ration cards and what risks are involved
- How Father responds to the possibility of being punished for helping Jewish people
- How faith shapes the ten Boom family's understanding of responsibility
- How fear is present, but does not have the final word in their decisions

What changes when fear is no longer distant or theoretical, but becomes attached to real people standing at the door?

### Overall Guiding Question ###

**As Nazi occupation begins to reshape daily life, how do the ten Booms choose faithfulness in the face of fear, and where do they see reminders of God’s care and provision?**
