# The Hiding Place: Forgiveness Over Fear #

## Chapters 13–15 and Epilog Reflection ##

Chapters 13–15 and the epilog of *The Hiding Place* show Corrie and Betsie facing the deepest suffering and fear of the book. Yet these chapters are not only about pain or terror. They also show gratitude, service, healing, forgiveness, and hope. Corrie's story does not present her as someone who finds faithfulness easy. She often struggles to see what Betsie sees, to feel what she believes, and to obey what she knows is right.

After reading Chapters 13–15 and the epilog, respond to the following prompt in a 1–2 page paper. Your paper should be no more than 3 pages. A typical response will be about 500–1,000 words, depending on formatting.

## Reflection Question ##

In Chapters 13–15 and the epilog, Corrie's suffering becomes part of a larger ministry of hope, healing, and forgiveness. But Corrie is not portrayed as someone who naturally rises above fear, anger, or weakness. How do these chapters show Corrie learning--often through God's provision and Betsie's example--to endure suffering, serve others, and choose forgiveness over fear, bitterness, or revenge?

In your response, discuss at least two specific examples from Chapters 13–15 and the epilog. You may consider questions such as:

- How does Betsie help Corrie see suffering, gratitude, and ministry differently?
- Where does Corrie struggle to respond faithfully?
- How do Scripture, prayer, and community help Corrie and Betsie endure Ravensbrück?
- How does Betsie's vision for healing go beyond helping only innocent victims?
- Why is it significant that Corrie's later ministry reaches many different groups?
- What does Corrie's struggle to forgive reveal about the difficulty of loving enemies?
- How does Corrie's weakness make her story more realistic and more spiritually meaningful?

Then connect these ideas to the larger course question: **How shall I live with respect to fear and security?**

You do not need to give a final answer to that question yet. Instead, use this paper to continue developing your answer. Chapters 13–15 and the epilog show that fear and suffering can wound people deeply, but they do not have the final word. Corrie's response to fear is not denial, bitterness, revenge, or despair. Instead, she gradually learns to receive God's strength for endurance, ministry, forgiveness, and hope. What do these chapters suggest about living faithfully when fear is justified, suffering is real, forgiveness is difficult, and hope must be received from God rather than produced by human strength?

You may include a brief connection to your own life, but you are not required to share anything deeply personal. If you do make a personal connection, focus on what you have learned about fear, suffering, forgiveness, hope, courage, weakness, or wise living. Feel free to include details from the lessons on theology and/or psychology.

Assume that your readers have not read the book. Your writing should include enough background and context to stand on its own.
