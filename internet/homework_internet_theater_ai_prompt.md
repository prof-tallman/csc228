# Human Thinking and AI Polishing #

Here is an example of how I used AI to help polish one of my own ideas for a homework assignment. The prompt below contains the ideas, requirements, technical content, and AI policy that I wanted the assignment to include. I then asked ChatGPT to organize and polish those ideas into a student-facing assignment.

Students who are interested may compare the AI-generated response with the [final assignment](https://github.com/prof-tallman/csc228/internet/homework_internet_theater.md), which is my own edited version of the AI's response.

**Note:** I had previously uploaded a handful of samples to ChatGPT so that it had access to the format, length, and tone of my work.

## My ChatGPT Prompt ##

OK, I have another assignment that I'm going to call "Internet Theater". Here's the basic idea. Please write this up in a style similar to my other GitHub assignments.

The students will be writing a short play that summarizes the important parts of the recent class material describing how the internet works. This material includes packetization, encapsulation, the layers of the TCP/IP model, routing, how TCP achieves reliability, and probably a little more. The play could be more of a high-level summary of all the material or it could get deeper into one particular topic. For example, a play about the layers of the TCP/IP model and encapsulation might have characters who represent each layer and they might pass things between them (aka data), adding and removing the various headers at each layer.

Some artwork is also required. It could be a poster advertising the play, or it could be an view of the stage mid-performance or a sketch/image of the main characters. Really, I just need a few visuals--say 3--that goes with the script itself.

Students are allowed to work in groups of up to 4. Groups must meet together and agree upon the basic idea of their play; afterwards, group members are encouraged to divide the work between themselves based on their own individual talents and interests. The quality of the work should scale by the group size: larger groups are expected to produce more than individuals.

Students who really like theater and scripts are welcome to write the entire thing themselves. But I assume that most students won't have the time or energy to spend on such creative thought midway through the semester. These students are allowed to use GenAI to write the script. *But* they need to explain to the AI the technical content that they want the play to represent. So the student is providing all of the technical material and then the AI is turning it into some sort of silly/cute/funny little play. They must restrict the play to the material that they understand and explain (students are welcome to do their own independent research--sans AI--before prompting the AI with the technical details).

Like the script itself, AI is allowed to generate the artwork.

Students must turn in their final script and artwork.

If AI was used for any part of the assignment, the AI prompts should be provided separately (this could be by sharing a link to the chat transcript or by copy-pasting the significant prompts into a separate document). These prompts should contain the core technical material, just in a form other than a theater script.
