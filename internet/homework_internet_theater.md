# Internet Theater #

The internet is built from many different systems working together. In class, we have covered some of those systems: messages are divided into packets, information is added and removed as data passes through the TCP/IP layers, routers move packets toward their destinations, and TCP helps unreliable networks behave reliably.

For this assignment, you will turn some of those ideas into a short play about how the internet works. Your play does not need to cover everything we have learned. You may create a broad overview of several ideas, or you may focus more deeply on one particular part of the process.

For example, a play about TCP/IP encapsulation might have characters representing the different layers of the TCP/IP model. As data moves from one character to another, each character might add the information needed by that layer. At the destination, the process could happen in reverse as the headers are removed and the original message is finally delivered. That is only one possibility. Be creative.

## Technical Content ##

Your play should accurately represent material that we have studied about how the internet works. Possible topics include: packetization, IP addresses, DNS, the layers of the TCP/IP model, routing and routing tables, the TCP protocol, and other closely related material from class.

You should **not** include every topic from this list. A good play might briefly connect several of these ideas together. Another good play might take one or two ideas and explore them in much greater detail.

The important requirement is that the technical ideas presented in your play are accurate and understandable.

## The Script ##

Write a short script that uses characters, dialogue, and action to explain the technical ideas you selected. Your play can be funny, dramatic, ridiculous, serious, or somewhere in between. You are not being graded on whether you are an expert playwright. You are being graded on whether the play demonstrates that you understand the computer science behind it.

You may personify technical components if that helps tell the story. Characters could include things such as:
- A web browser who really wants to see pictures of cute puppies
- A server or router trying to faithfully fulfill its duties
- The Application, Transport, Internet, or Network Access layers
- A lost packet desperately trying to find its way home

## Artwork ##

You must create at least three pieces of artwork to accompany your play. The artwork should clearly connect to your play rather than simply being generic pictures of computers or the internet. It might include:
- A poster advertising the play
- A drawing of the stage during a scene
- Character sketches
- A diagram incorporated into the fictional world of the play
- Other artwork that helps bring your script to life

## Working in Groups ##

You may complete this assignment individually or in a group of up to four students. The expected scope and quality of the project increases with group size. This is not a strict one-to-one scaling problem where a three-person group needs to produce three times as much work as a single student. However, a four-person group should produce a more substantial project than somebody working alone.

If you work in a group, your group must first meet together and agree on the basic idea for the play and the technical material that it will explain. After you have agreed on the overall direction, you are encouraged to divide the work according to your individual interests and talents. For example, one person might enjoy developing characters, another might work on dialogue, another might concentrate on checking the technical content, and another might create artwork. You do not all need to contribute to every part of the project, but every group member should understand the technical ideas represented in the final play.

## Using Generative AI ##

Generative AI **is allowed** for this assignment, including for both the script and the artwork. However, there is an important restriction:

> **You must provide the technical content. AI may help turn your technical understanding into a play, but it may not supply that understanding for you.**

Before asking AI to write your script, you should decide what your play is going to teach and explain those ideas to the AI yourself. For example, you might explain:
- what happens when an application wants to contact a web server,
- how the data moves through the TCP/IP layers,
- what information each layer adds,
- what routers do with IP packets,
- or how TCP uses sequence numbers and acknowledgements.

I expect your prompts to contain a similar level of substantive technical content and understanding. The AI may help with the creative transformation, but the computer science should come from you.

You should restrict the play to technical material that you understand well enough to explain in your prompt. For example, here is a [link](homework_internet_theater_ai_prompt.md) to the prompt that I wrote for this very assignment. As you'll see, I designed the assignment myself and then asked AI to generate something in the same format and style as my own previous work. I expect your prompts to contain the same level of core technical content and understanding.

You are welcome to conduct additional research before writing your prompt, but that research should be done without using generative AI to explain the material for you. You should understand the material first and then use AI as a creative tool.

You are also responsible for checking anything AI produces. If the AI introduces incorrect technical information or adds concepts that you do not understand, revise or remove them.

### AI Artwork ###

Generative AI may also be used to create any or all of the required artwork.

Your group should still decide what the artwork should depict and how it connects to the play.

### AI Documentation ###

If you use generative AI for any part of this assignment, you must also submit the significant prompts that you used. You may do this by providing a shareable link to the relevant AI conversation, or copying the important prompts into a separate document. You do not need to submit every tiny correction or follow-up prompt.

I am particularly interested in the prompts that describe the technical content of your play. Those prompts should demonstrate your understanding of the internet concepts before they were transformed into characters, dialogue, jokes, and theatrical scenes. In other words, I should be able to read your prompts and see the computer science behind the play.

## What to Submit ##

Submit:

3. Your AI prompts or conversation link, if generative AI was used

If you worked in a group, clearly list the names of all group members.

## What I Am Looking For ##

A strong submission will:

* Accurately explain important ideas about how the internet works
* Demonstrate genuine understanding of the technical material
* Turn those ideas into a coherent and creative theatrical concept
* Use dialogue, characters, or action to help communicate the concepts
* Include artwork that meaningfully complements the script
* Show an appropriate amount of effort for the number of students in the group
* Clearly document the technical information given to generative AI, if AI was used

The goal is not to discover who in the class is secretly a brilliant playwright. Instead, the goal is to demonstrate that you understand how the internet works well enough to turn its invisible processes into a story that someone else could see, hear, and understand.
