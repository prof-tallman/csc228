# DNS Address Book Activity Group Questions #

We just completed an activity that translated between human-readable names (people) and computer-based IP addresses (numbers). The activity went something like this:

As students enter the room, give each one of them a slip of paper containing an IP address. Their goal is to create and maintain a written list of every one of their classmates' IP addresses. The only rules were:

- Students may walk around the room.
- Students may share information with classmates.
- Students may talk or share information with only one classmate at a time.
- Students must write the IP address list on a piece of paper.

As the students worked, I circulated quietly through the room and exchanged their IP address slip for another. My goal was to make it increasingly difficult for the students to maintain an accurate list.

## Discussion Questions ##

1. Why do you think I was switching your IP addresses?
<br><br><br><br>

2. What made the exercise difficult: the number of classmates, the changing IP addresses, or the fact that information spread person-to-person? Why?
<br><br><br><br>

3. If this activity had 30 students, it would be annoying. If it had 30 million devices, what new problems would appear?
<br><br><br><br>

4. What could go wrong if someone gave you the wrong IP address for a website?
<br><br><br><br>

5. If IP addresses can change, is there a better way for everyone to know everyone else's IP address?
