# Sherlock Holmes: Reading Guide for Select Stories #

## Focus Points for "The Red-Headed League" ##

### An Absurd Mystery Hiding a Serious Crime ###

*The Red-Headed League* begins with a situation that seems ridiculous: Jabez Wilson is paid well to copy from the Encyclopaedia Britannica simply because he has bright red hair. Holmes, however, treats the strange details seriously and recognizes that the absurd story may be hiding a dangerous crime. As you read, pay attention to how Holmes separates distracting details from meaningful clues. Look especially for:

- Why Jabez Wilson comes to Holmes for help
- How Wilson first hears about the Red-Headed League
- What work Wilson is hired to do
- Why the job seems suspicious or unusual
- Why Wilson's employee, Vincent Spaulding, is important to the mystery
- What Holmes notices about Spaulding's appearance and behavior
- Why the location of Wilson's shop matters
- How the strange job connects to the planned crime

**Modern security connection:** Notice how the criminals use distraction, trust, routine, and a fake opportunity to move Wilson away from what they really want. How do modern cyberattacks use similar misleading information or confusion?

How does Holmes recognize that an absurd situation may be hiding a serious plan?

## Focus Points for "Silver Blaze" ##

### Missing Evidence and the Silence of the Watchdog ###

*Silver Blaze* begins with the disappearance of a famous racehorse and the suspicious death of its trainer, John Straker. The case appears to involve theft, murder, and several obvious suspects, but Holmes pays close attention to what did *not* happen as much as to what did. As you read, pay attention to how Holmes uses absence, behavior, and motive as evidence. Look especially for:

- What happened to John Straker
- Why Fitzroy Simpson seems like an obvious suspect
- What evidence appears to point toward Simpson
- Why the stable boy's condition matters
- Why the dog's silence is important
- What Holmes notices about the sheep
- How Holmes explains Straker's actions
- Why the horse was hidden in plain sight

**Modern security connection:** Notice how Holmes treats missing alerts, unusual behavior, and false assumptions as important evidence. In modern security, a system's failure to react can sometimes be just as meaningful as an obvious alarm.

How does Holmes use what *did not happen* to understand what really happened?

## Focus Points for "The Speckled Band" ##

### Misleading Clues and Careful Observation ###

*The Speckled Band* begins when Helen Stoner asks Holmes to investigate the mysterious death of her sister, Julia. The story includes several clues that seem to point in one direction, but Holmes notices details that other people overlook. As you read, pay attention to the difference between the explanation that seems obvious and the explanation supported by careful observation. Look especially for:

- Why Helen Stoner comes to Holmes
- What happened to Julia Stoner before her death
- What Julia's last words were
- What makes Dr. Roylott seem suspicious
- Why the gypsies seem like a possible explanation
- What Holmes notices about the bedroom
- Why the ventilator, bell-rope, and bed matter
- How Holmes discovers the real meaning of "the speckled band"

**Modern security connection:** Notice how the crime depends on access, physical design, insider control, and misleading evidence. How might these same factors create weaknesses in modern cybersecurity?

How does Holmes avoid being misled by the most obvious explanation?

## Overall Guiding Question ##

**Overall Guiding Question: How can careful observation and clear reasoning reveal security weaknesses hidden by trust, distraction, or misleading appearances?**
