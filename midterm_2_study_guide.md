# Midterm #2: Preparing for the Exam #

This exam will assess more than your ability to remember names, terms, or isolated facts. You will be asked to explain important ideas in your own words, apply them to specific examples, connect technical knowledge with questions about fear and security, and bring together ideas from *Beyond Fear* and *The Hiding Place*.

## Overview ##

The exam will include two parts:

- **Part I: Technical Concepts** will ask you to explain and apply ideas from *Beyond Fear* and from our class activities about how the internet works. You will need to write three paragraphs, each one about a different topic.
- **Part II: The Fear Essay** will ask you to use *Beyond Fear* and *The Hiding Place* to develop a thoughtful response to a question about fear, security, judgment, and the treatment of others. You should expect to write approximately 2 pages, all about the same topic.

You will not know the exact questions in advance. Prepare by making sure that you understand the major ideas well enough to explain them without relying on memorized definitions or prepared paragraphs.

### Readings Covered ###

* *Beyond Fear*, Chapters 1, 2, 3, 7, and 13
* *The Hiding Place*, Chapters 1–8

### Suggested Use of Time ###

* Part I: 30 minutes
* Part II: 45 minutes

### Quotes Page ###

You may bring an approved notes page containing three selected quotations from each book. Choose quotations that:

- You understand well.
- Represent important ideas or decisions.
- Could support more than one possible argument.
- Help you connect the two books.

For each quotation, know its context, who said it or what idea it expresses, and why it matters. The quotation itself will not earn much credit unless you explain how it supports your response.

### Homework Page ###

In addition to your quotes page, you may print and bring one completed homework assignment from this course. The assignment must be your own work and must remain in its original form; you may not add new notes, outlines, quotations, or a prepared essay to it before the exam.

Choose a homework assignment that will help you recall important ideas, examples, or connections from the course. You may refer to it during either part of the exam, but you will still need to explain the material in your own words and apply it to the questions you are given.

## Part I: Technical Concepts ##

**40 points**

Review the following ideas from *Beyond Fear*:

- The difference between a threat and a risk
- How people estimate and misjudge risk
- Security theater
- The attacker’s advantage
- Complexity and its effects on security
- Authentication, identity, and trust
- The costs, benefits, and tradeoffs involved in security decisions

Review the major ideas from our internet activities:

- How information is divided into packets
- How the layers of TCP/IP model work together through encapsulation
- The roles of addresses, networks, routers, and routing tables
- How routers move packets toward their destinations
- How TCP detects and responds to lost, delayed, duplicated, or out-of-order packets
- Why technical knowledge can help a person evaluate security claims more accurately

Be prepared to:

- Explain a concept or process clearly in your own words.
- Use a specific example from a reading or class activity.
- Describe the important steps in a technical process.
- Connect an internet concept to a question about fear, risk, trust, or security.

## Part II: Essay on Fear ##

**60 points**

Part II will ask you to write a short essay that brings together *Beyond Fear* and *The Hiding Place*. You will not be asked simply to summarize the books. Instead, you will use ideas and examples from both books to answer a question about how people should respond when fear influences their judgment, decisions, or treatment of others.

As you prepare, review:

- How Schneier distinguishes fear, threats, risks, and security.
- How fear can lead people to misjudge danger or support ineffective or excessive security measures.
- The ideas of security theater, tradeoffs, complexity, the attacker’s advantage, authentication, and trust.
- Important decisions made by Corrie, Betsie, Nollie, and others in Chapters 1–8 of *The Hiding Place*.
- Examples of people responding to fear with courage, preparation, honesty, deception, trust, love, resistance, or faith.
- Situations in which protecting oneself may conflict with protecting or caring for another person.
- The principles that you think should guide a person when fear feels greater than the actual danger.

Be prepared to:

- State a clear answer to the essay question.
- Use at least **one important idea** from *Beyond Fear*.
- Discuss at least **one specific event**, person, or decision from *The Hiding Place*.
- Use and explain at least **one quotation from each book**.
- Connect the two books rather than discussing them separately.
- Recognize a tension, limitation, cost, or reasonable alternative to your position.
- End with a principle that could guide your own future decisions.
