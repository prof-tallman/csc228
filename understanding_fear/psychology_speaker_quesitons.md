# Guest Speaker Question Preparation #

## Overview ##

A guest speaker will visit our class to help us think about fear from a scientific, medical, psychological, or counseling perspective. Your goal is to come prepared with thoughtful questions to help the class learn more from the guest speaker.

## Purpose ##

This assignment is meant to help you:

- Prepare for the guest speaker's visit
- Connect the speaker's expertise to the themes of this course
- Contribute to an interesting class discussion or Q&A

## Instructions ##

Before the guest speaker visits class, prepare **five thoughtful questions** that you could ask during the presentation or Q&A.

Your questions may focus on topics such as:

- What fear is and how it works
- How fear affects the mind, body, emotions, or behavior
- Why different people respond to fear in different ways
- How fear can be helpful, harmful, rational, or irrational
- How doctors, psychologists, counselors, or therapists help people face fear
- How people can learn to manage or overcome fear
- How fear relates to security, risk, technology, safety, or decision-making
- How people can respond wisely when they feel afraid

At least **one** of your questions should make a clear connection to the themes of this course: technology, security, fear, risk, safety, or wise decision-making.

You do **not** need to ask all of your questions out loud. These questions are meant to help you prepare. If there is time during Q&A, you should be ready to ask one of them.

## Optional Anonymous Question ##

If one of your questions is important to you but you would rather not ask it publicly, please mark it as such. Prof. Tallman may choose to ask these anonymous student questions during the Q&A.

## Examples of Stronger Questions

- What is one common misunderstanding people have about fear or anxiety?
- How can someone tell the difference between a reasonable fear and a fear that is controlling them?
- In security fields, people often have to think about worst-case scenarios. How can someone do that responsibly without becoming consumed by fear?

## Examples of Weaker Questions

- What is fear?
- Do people get scared?
- What do you do for your job?

These questions are not necessarily wrong, but they are too broad or too general. Stronger questions usually show that you have already started thinking about the topic.
